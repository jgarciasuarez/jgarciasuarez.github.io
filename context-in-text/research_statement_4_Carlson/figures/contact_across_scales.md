# Contact Across Scales

This figure illustrates the phenomena of contact and friction across various length scales, structured along a logarithmic axis from $10^{-9}$ to $10^3$ meters. It is divided into three main panels:

1. **Nanoscale Wear ($10^{-9}$ to $10^{-6}$ m):** Focuses on the atomic and micro scale, highlighting mechanisms like shear, plasticity, and fracture at the contact interface between rough surfaces. It includes graphs comparing measured versus predicted wear volumes.
2. **Haptics ($10^{-3}$ to $10^0$ m):** Depicts human-scale interaction, showing a finger touching a surface. It features a graph of contact radius versus velocity, and diagrams illustrating fluid dynamics effects like air bubble entrapment causing contact area loss due to elasticity, inertia, and viscosity over microsecond timescales.
3. **Data-Driven Friction ($10^0$ to $10^3$ m):** Focuses on macro-scale phenomena like tectonic fault lines. It shows a block diagram of sliding faults and a plot of rate-and-state friction data, comparing training targets with predictions under an evolving sliding velocity protocol.
