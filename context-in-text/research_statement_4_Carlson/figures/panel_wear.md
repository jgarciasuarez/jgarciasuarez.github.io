# Panel: Nanoscale Wear

This figure focuses on "nanoscale wear" phenomena occurring at length scales from $10^{-9}$ to $10^{-6}$ meters.

The panel includes:
- A cross-sectional diagram of two rough surfaces sliding against each other under shear. It highlights key contact mechanics questions by pointing to regions of "contact" and asking where "plasticity?" and "fracture?" dominate. An inset shows a macro-scale gear analogy.
- Two scatter plots related to wear volume ($V_d$ [nm$^3$]).
  - The left plot shows a strong linear correlation between "measured $V_d$" and "predicted $V_d$," comparing new data with previous models (Aghababaei, 2017).
  - The right plot shows "measured $V_d$" on a logarithmic scale against energy/work ($W_f$ [keV]), indicating power-law scaling relationships (e.g., $\sim W_f$ and $\sim W_f^{3/2}$) with corresponding simulation snapshots of debris formation insets.
- Reference: G.S. et al., JMPS (2022).
